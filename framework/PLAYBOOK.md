# The Newsletter Playbook

The system for turning what you think into something people want to read, and for learning email marketing as you go.

---

## 1. The foundation (decide once, revisit every 3 months)

### Why this exists
Write one sentence and keep it at the top of your mind:

> *"I write this to think more clearly about how to live, and to share what I find with the people I care about."*

Change the words, but keep it short. When you're stuck on what to write, go back to this sentence.

### Who it's for, right now
Your first readers are family, close friends and relatives. That's a real advantage:
- **They already trust you.** You don't need to prove anything. You only need to be honest and worth their time.
- **They'll reply.** Replies are where you learn most, and they're the strongest signal to email providers that your mail isn't spam.
- **It's low-stakes practice.** Every skill you'll need later (subject lines, structure, consistency, list growth) can be learned with 20 readers.

Write each issue to **one specific person** in that circle. Pick someone different each time if that helps. "You" in a newsletter should feel like a letter, not an announcement.

### Your content pillars (3 to 4)
Pillars keep the newsletter recognizable and stop you from staring at a blank page. Starter set, which you can rename in the app:

| Pillar | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **Philosophy** | How you see life, beliefs you hold, beliefs you've changed |
| **Learning** | Books, ideas, skills, mental models you're working through |
| **Living** | Habits, relationships, work, health: philosophy put into practice |
| **Noticing** | Small observations, moments, things that made you stop |

Every idea you capture gets one pillar. Over time you'll see which ones you write best and which ones readers respond to.

### The name
A good name is short, easy to say out loud, and hints at the feeling rather than the topic. Patterns that work:
- **Object + you:** *Notes from [Name]*, *[Name]'s Margin Notes*
- **Metaphor:** *Field Notes*, *The Long Way Round*, *Slow Thoughts*
- **Invitation:** *Worth Thinking About*, *Sunday Letters*

Don't overthink it. You can rename it at issue 10 and nobody will mind.

## 2. The format (same shape every issue)

Readers return to formats they recognize. Use the same skeleton every time so you only decide **what** to say, never **how** to lay it out.

1. **Subject line**: specific, curious, honest. Under about 50 characters.
2. **Preview text**: the line that shows in the inbox next to the subject. Never leave it blank.
3. **Opening note** (2 to 4 sentences): what's going on in your life right now. Human, warm, a bit casual.
4. **The big idea** (300 to 700 words): **one** idea, explored properly. The heart of the issue.
5. **What I'm learning** (1 to 3 bullets): a book, a concept, a skill in progress, and what it taught you.
6. **Worth your time** (1 to 3 links): each with *one sentence on why*. A link without a reason is noise.
7. **A question for you**: one real question tied to the big idea. Ask for replies.
8. **Sign-off**: the same every time. It becomes your signature.

**Target length:** 4 to 6 minutes of reading. Shorter is fine. Longer needs to earn it.

---

## 3. The rhythm: one issue every two weeks

**Every two weeks** is the starting point. Weekly burns people out before the habit forms. Monthly is too far apart for anyone to remember you. Pick a **fixed day** (Sunday morning works well for personal newsletters) and keep it. Consistency builds trust faster than anything else you can do.

### The two-week cycle

| When | Step | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Always** | Capture | 30 sec per idea | Put any thought, quote, question or moment into the Idea Inbox. Don't judge it. |
| **Week 1, any day** | Choose | 15 min | Review the inbox. Pick the one idea with the most energy. Move it to *Ready*. |
| **Week 1** | Draft | 60 to 90 min | Write the big idea fast and badly. Fill the rest of the template. |
| **Week 2, early** | Rest | 1 to 2 days | Don't touch it. Distance makes editing easier. |
| **Week 2** | Edit | 30 to 45 min | Run the editing checklist (below). Cut 20%. |
| **Send day** | Send | 10 min | Send a test to yourself first, then send. |
| **2 to 3 days after** | Reflect | 10 min | Log opens, clicks and replies. Answer every reply. Note one lesson. |

About **3 hours every two weeks**. That's sustainable for years, and lasting years matters more than anything else in this playbook.

### The idea pipeline
Ideas move through three stages:
- 🌱 **Seed**: a raw thought, one line. Most ideas stay here, and that's fine.
- 🌿 **Sprouting**: you've added a few notes, an example, a "so what".
- 🌳 **Ready**: you could write 500 words on it today.

**Rule of thumb:** keep at least 3 ideas in *Sprouting* and you'll never face a blank page.

## 4. Writing it well

### Turn a thought into an essay
Use this shape for the big idea when you're stuck:
1. **The moment**: a specific scene, conversation or observation that started the thought.
2. **The tension**: what seemed true before, and what's bothering you about it now.
3. **The insight**: what you've come to believe, and why.
4. **The practice**: what this changes in how you actually live.
5. **The open edge**: what you're still unsure about. Honesty here is what makes people reply.

### Principles
- **One issue, one idea.** If you have two good ideas, you have two issues.
- **Specific beats general.** *"My dad fixing the fence at 6am in the rain"* beats *"hard work matters"*.
- **Show your thinking, not just your conclusions.** People follow a mind, not a list of opinions.
- **Admit uncertainty.** "I'm not sure about this yet" is an invitation. Certainty closes the conversation.
- **Write like you talk**, then cut the filler.

### The editing checklist (run it before every send)
- [ ] Can I state the big idea in **one sentence**?
- [ ] Does the first line make someone want to read the second?
- [ ] Did I cut at least 20%?
- [ ] Is there a **specific story or example**, not only abstractions?
- [ ] Does every link have a *why*?
- [ ] Is there **one clear question** inviting replies?
- [ ] Subject line: would *I* open this from someone else?
- [ ] Preview text filled in, not left to show "View in browser"?
- [ ] Read it **out loud** once. Fix anything that made me stumble.
- [ ] Sent a **test email to myself** and checked it on my phone?

## 5. Subject lines

The subject line decides whether anything else gets read. Formulas that suit a personal newsletter:

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| **The honest admission** | *I was wrong about ambition* |
| **The specific number** | *3 things my grandmother knew about money* |
| **The question** | *What are you optimizing for?* |
| **The unexpected pairing** | *Gardening and grief* |
| **The plain topic** (for loyal readers) | *On slowing down* |

**Avoid:** clickbait you can't deliver on, ALL CAPS, "Newsletter #7" as the whole subject, and spammy words (*free*, *act now*, *!!!*), which also hurt deliverability.

Write **three** subject lines for every issue and pick the best. Later, when your tool supports A/B tests, test two of them.

## 6. Tools and setup

### Pick one email platform
You want: free to start, good deliverability, simple analytics (opens, clicks), and room to grow. Any of these is a solid choice:

| Platform | Why you'd choose it |
|---|---|
| **Kit** (formerly ConvertKit) | Industry-standard *email marketing* tool. Learn tags, sequences and automations properly. Free tier for small lists. |
| **Beehiiv** | Built for newsletters, with good growth tools (referrals, recommendations). Free tier. |
| **Buttondown** | Minimal, markdown-friendly, made for writers. Small free tier. |
| **Substack** | Easiest to start, with a built-in web archive and discovery. Less control and less "marketing" learning. |

**Recommendation for your goal of learning email marketing: Kit or Beehiiv.** They teach the concepts (segments, automations, welcome sequences, landing pages) you'll use when you take this further.

> Please **don't** send from your personal Gmail with everyone in BCC once you're past about 15 people. You get no analytics, no unsubscribe link, and a much higher chance of landing in spam.

### Setup checklist
- [ ] Create the account and set the sender name to **your real name** (people open mail from people).
- [ ] Use a reply-to address you actually read.
- [ ] Write the **welcome email** that goes out automatically to every new subscriber (template in `launch-invite.md`).
- [ ] Create a simple **signup page** (every platform has one) so people can forward it.
- [ ] *Later:* a custom domain for sending (e.g. `hello@yourname.com`) improves deliverability and looks professional.

### Permission matters
Even with family: **ask before you add people.** Send a personal invite (template in `launch-invite.md`) and only add those who say yes. It's respectful, it's the law in many places (CAN-SPAM, GDPR), and people who opted in actually read.

## 7. Measure, learn, improve

You're doing this partly to *learn* email marketing, so treat every issue as a small experiment. Log these in the **Issue Log** in the app:

| Metric | What it tells you | Healthy for a personal list |
|---|---|---|
| **Open rate** | Did the subject line and your name earn attention? | 50 to 70%+ (close circles open a lot) |
| **Click rate** | Did the links feel worth it? | 5 to 15% |
| **Replies** | Did it make people *feel* something? | **The most important number.** Aim for 1 or more every issue. |
| **Unsubscribes** | Is it a fit? | Some is normal and healthy. Don't take it personally. |
| **Forwards / new subs** | Is it worth sharing? | Your real growth signal |

> **Note:** Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates open rates. Treat opens as a rough trend, and trust replies and clicks more.

### The one-lesson rule
After every issue, write **one sentence**: *"Next time I'll ___."* After 10 issues you'll have 10 lessons that are specific to you. That's worth more than any course.

### Every quarter (every 6 issues), ask:
- Which 2 issues got the most replies? What did they have in common?
- Which pillar do I most enjoy writing? Which do readers respond to?
- Is the cadence sustainable? Adjust if needed. Changing the plan beats quitting.
- Send a **short reader survey** (3 questions max): *What do you look forward to? What would you skip? What should I write about?*

## 8. Making it something people want to follow

What makes a personal newsletter matter to people:

1. **Generosity.** Every issue should leave the reader with something they can use: a question, a reframe, a book, a practice.
2. **Vulnerability with a point.** Share struggles, but always with what you learned or are still working out. Don't just vent.
3. **Conversation, not broadcast.** Answer every reply. Quote (with permission) a reader's reply in a later issue: *"Last time I asked X. Here's what you said."* That one habit makes readers feel like participants.
4. **Running threads.** Callbacks to earlier issues reward loyal readers and build a sense of a shared story.
5. **Consistency.** Showing up on the same day every time is a quiet promise that you keep.

## 9. Growth path (when you're ready to go beyond your circle)

Don't start here. Earn it with about **10 consistent issues** first.

| Stage | Size | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **1: Circle** | 10 to 50 | Find your voice. Build the habit. Get replies. |
| **2: Friends of friends** | 50 to 250 | Add a "forward this to someone" line. Put the signup link in your social bios. Start a public archive. |
| **3: Public** | 250 to 1,000 | Post excerpts on one social platform. Do recommendation swaps with similar newsletters. Build a 3-email welcome sequence. |
| **4: Audience** | 1,000+ | Consider a lead magnet, sponsorships or a paid tier, but only if it serves the readers. |

Each stage adds **one** new growth tactic. Learn it properly before adding the next.

## 10. Your first 30 days

- [ ] **Day 1:** Write your one-sentence *why*. Pick a working name. Choose 3 to 4 pillars.
- [ ] **Days 1 to 7:** Capture at least **15 ideas** in the Idea Inbox. Quantity over quality.
- [ ] **Day 3:** Make a list of 15 to 30 people. Send personal invites (`launch-invite.md`).
- [ ] **Day 5:** Set up your email platform and welcome email.
- [ ] **Days 7 to 10:** Write **Issue #1** (a starter draft is already in the app: *why I'm starting this*).
- [ ] **Day 10:** Send a test to yourself, run the checklist, then send it.
- [ ] **Days 12 to 13:** Answer every reply. Log the stats. Write your one lesson.
- [ ] **Days 14 to 24:** Write and send **Issue #2** using the two-week cycle.
- [ ] **Day 30:** Look back at both issues. What felt most like *you*? Do more of that.
